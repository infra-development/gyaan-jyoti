# Story Time

## The Village That Worked Together

In a small village, everyone had a special job.

Rohan grew vegetables.

Mina stitched clothes.

Asha taught children.

Rahul repaired bicycles.

Grandfather made beautiful wooden chairs.

One morning, Rohan had an idea.

> "Why don't we all do every job ourselves?"

Everyone agreed to try.

Soon problems appeared.

The vegetables were not ready.

The clothes looked untidy.

The chairs were weak.

The bicycles stayed broken.

Children had no teacher.

At the end of the day, the village had less food, fewer clothes, and many unhappy people.

Grandfather smiled and said,

> **"When everyone tries to do everything, nobody becomes excellent at anything."**

The next day, everyone returned to their own work.

The village became happy again.

# New Concepts

## Goods

**Goods** are physical things that people make, grow, or build.

You can usually **see**, **touch**, and **use** them.

Examples:

* Rice
* Bread
* Bicycle
* Book
* School Bag
* Shirt

---

## Services

**Services** are work people do to help others.

You cannot take a service home in a bag, but it makes life better.

Examples:

* Teaching
* Haircut
* Medical treatment
* Bus driving
* Repairing a fan
* Cleaning a classroom

---

## Producer

A **producer** is someone who creates goods or provides services.

Examples:

* Farmer
* Doctor
* Teacher
* Carpenter
* Tailor
* Barber

---

## Consumer

A **consumer** is someone who uses goods or services.

Everyone is a consumer because everyone uses things made or provided by others.

---

## Specialization

**Specialization** means focusing on one type of work and becoming very good at it.

Examples:

* Doctors treat patients.
* Farmers grow crops.
* Teachers teach students.
* Bakers bake bread.
* Carpenters build furniture.

When people specialize, they usually become faster, more skilled, and produce better results.

# Parent Notes (For Adults)

Today's goal is not to memorize definitions.

Help your child observe the real world.

Whenever you use something during the day, ask:

* Is this a good or a service?
* Who produced it?
* Could our family make it ourselves?

This will naturally prepare the child for tomorrow's lesson on **trade**.
