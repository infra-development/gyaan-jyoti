# Big Question

> **If different people make different things, how does everyone get what they need?**

# Lesson Theme

Yesterday, you learned that different people produce different **goods** and **services**.

But one important question remains.

If a farmer grows vegetables and a tailor stitches clothes, how does the farmer get clothes? And how does the tailor get vegetables?

Today, we will discover how humans solved this problem through **trade**.

# Story Time

## The First Trade

Long ago, there was no money.

A farmer named Ravi grew wheat.

A potter named Meena made clay pots.

A tailor named Arjun stitched clothes.

One day, Ravi needed new clothes.

He visited Arjun.

"I don't have clothes," Ravi said, "but I have plenty of wheat."

Arjun smiled.

"I need wheat for my family."

So Ravi gave Arjun a bag of wheat.

Arjun stitched new clothes for Ravi.

Both of them went home happy.

A few days later, Meena needed vegetables.

She exchanged clay pots with another farmer.

Soon, the entire village was exchanging things.

Nobody called it "trade."

They simply helped one another by exchanging what they had for what they needed.

Without realizing it, the village had discovered one of humanity's greatest ideas.

**Trade.**

# New Concepts

## Exchange

An **exchange** means giving something and receiving something else in return.

Examples:

* Giving vegetables for rice.
* Helping someone repair a bicycle in return for fresh milk.
* Exchanging books with a friend.

Exchange happens when both people agree.

---

## Trade

**Trade** is the exchange of goods or services between people.

Trade helps everyone get things they cannot easily produce themselves.

Examples:

* A farmer gives vegetables to a baker.
* A baker gives bread to a teacher.
* A tailor stitches clothes for customers.

Trade allows everyone to enjoy many different goods and services.

---

## Fair Trade

A trade should be **fair**.

Both people should feel that they received something valuable.

If only one person benefits, the trade is probably not fair.

# Parent Notes (For Adults)

Avoid introducing money yet.

Today's lesson is about understanding **why trade naturally emerged**, not how people paid for things.

Let your child discover that trade is simply people helping one another by exchanging value.

Money will be introduced gradually in future weeks.
