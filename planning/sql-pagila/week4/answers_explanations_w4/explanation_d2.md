# Introduction

In Day 1, you learned that Window Functions perform calculations without removing rows.

However, every calculation was performed across the **entire dataset**.

For example:

- Overall average payment
- Overall revenue
- Overall payment count

Businesses rarely analyze the entire company as one large group.

Instead, they ask questions like:

- How much has **each customer** spent?
- What is the average payment **for each customer**?
- How many customers belong to **each store**?
- What is the average rental duration **for each film rating**?

To answer these questions, we must divide the data into smaller analytical groups.

That is the purpose of `PARTITION BY`.

---

# What is PARTITION BY?

`PARTITION BY` divides rows into logical groups.

Each group is analyzed independently.

Unlike `GROUP BY`:

- No rows are removed.
- Every original row remains visible.

General syntax:

```sql
Function(...)
OVER (
    PARTITION BY column_name
)
```

Think of a partition as a temporary analytical group.

# Concept 1 - Customer Revenue (Q11)

## Business Question

Display every payment together with the total revenue generated by that customer.

Example:

| Customer | Payment | Customer Total |
|----------|--------:|---------------:|
| Mary | 2.99 | 74.91 |
| Mary | 5.99 | 74.91 |
| Mary | 4.99 | 74.91 |

Notice:

Every payment remains visible.

The total revenue is repeated for each payment made by that customer.

This is analytical reporting.

# Concept 7 - GROUP BY vs PARTITION BY (Q19)

These two clauses solve different problems.

## GROUP BY

```
Input

100 Rows

↓

Output

10 Rows
```

Rows are grouped together.

Only summaries remain.

---

## PARTITION BY

```
Input

100 Rows

↓

Output

100 Rows
```

Rows stay exactly as they were.

Only analytical values are added.

This is the biggest conceptual difference.

# Concept 8 - Thinking with PARTITION BY (Q18 & Q20)

Before writing SQL,

ask:

## What is my business group?

Examples:

- Customer
- Store
- Film Rating
- Category

Next ask:

Should calculations restart for each group?

If yes,

use:

```sql
PARTITION BY
```

---

# GROUP BY vs PARTITION BY

| GROUP BY | PARTITION BY |
|----------|--------------|
| Reduces rows | Preserves rows |
| Produces summary rows | Adds analytical values |
| One row per group | Every row remains |
| Used for summaries | Used for analytical reporting |

---

# Common Mistakes

❌ Thinking `PARTITION BY` groups rows like `GROUP BY`.

❌ Forgetting that every row remains visible.

❌ Partitioning by the wrong business entity.

❌ Using `GROUP BY` when row-level detail is still required.

❌ Choosing a partition that doesn't match the business question.

# Day 2 Summary

Today you learned:

- `PARTITION BY` divides data into logical analytical groups.
- Every partition is analyzed independently.
- Calculations restart for each partition.
- Every original row remains visible.
- `PARTITION BY` is fundamentally different from `GROUP BY`.
- Correct partition selection depends on the business requirement.
