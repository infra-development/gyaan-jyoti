# SQL Design Checklist

Before writing any complex SQL query, I ask myself:

☐ What business problem am I solving?

☐ What business entities are involved?

☐ What business metrics are required?

☐ Can this problem be divided into smaller steps?

☐ Which SQL technique best fits each step?

☐ Can intermediate calculations be reused?

☐ Are meaningful names assigned to every CTE?

☐ Is my SQL easy to read?

☐ Can another developer understand it?

☐ Can I explain every line of SQL?

# Week 5 Success Criteria

You are ready for **Week 6 – Business Analytics & Reporting** if you can confidently:

☐ Break large business requirements into smaller SQL components.

☐ Build complex SQL solutions without becoming overwhelmed.

# Final Reflection

If you can complete this checklist honestly without referring back to the lessons, you've achieved the primary objective of Week 5.

You are no longer approaching SQL as a collection of commands.

You are approaching it as a **problem-solving and design language**, capable of breaking complex business requirements into clear, logical, reusable, and maintainable solutions.
